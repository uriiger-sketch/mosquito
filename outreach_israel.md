## Before you send

1. **Open the link on your own phone first.** Tap START and make sure it runs.
2. **Check the server.** If `https://mosquito.charity/health` is down, the app still detects on the phone, but the top bar shows OFFLINE and the global map has no live data. Researchers will notice.
3. **Send each email separately from your own address.** No BCC blast: a personal note to six people is collaboration, a mass mailing is spam.
4. **Don't overstate results.** The long-range numbers come from simulated recordings, not live mosquitoes. The drafts say so, and that gap is the honest reason to ask for help.
5. **Known gap to raise with them:** the app doesn't yet include *Culex perexiguus*. The Ministry of Health's own research names it the main West Nile vector in Israel.

**Subject:** Phone-based acoustic mosquito detection: could we test it on your colonies?

Dear Dr. Bohbot,

I'm developing MosquitoNet, a free, open-source web app that detects mosquitoes by their wingbeat tone using an ordinary phone microphone. All processing happens on the phone and no audio is stored. Your group's YOLito work showed how much a shared, general detector can do for image-based tracking. I'm trying to do something similar for sound, so people in high-risk areas can monitor with nothing more than a phone.

The detector now uses a sonar-style tracker that, in simulation, works at signal levels about 26 dB weaker than before. That could mean tens of centimetres instead of a few. ## Email 3 — Dr. Yaniv Lustig

**Subject:** Free phone-based mosquito monitoring: could it complement national WNV surveillance?

Dear Dr. Lustig,

I'm developing MosquitoNet, a free, open-source web app that listens for mosquito wingbeat tones through a phone microphone. It identifies likely vector species on the device and reports confirmed detections to a map, with location blurred by about 2.8 km for privacy. After the early rise of West Nile fever in June 2024, I wondered whether many phones could act as a cheap, dense early-warning layer alongside the trap network.

I'm not suggesting it replaces traps. Acoustic identification can't confirm virus. But I'd value your view on whether this is useful to public health, and whether the detections could one day be compared with your trap data for validation. If the Laboratory of Entomology would be the right partner, I'd be very grateful for an introduction.
