          About MosquitoNet

          Hear the mosquito
          before it bites.

          MosquitoNet listens for mosquito wingbeats, names the likely species on your phone, and adds confirmed finds — anonymously — to a global map of disease vectors.

          How detection works

          Each species beats its wings at its own pitch — about 290–730 Hz, with overtones at two and three times that. MosquitoNet looks for steady tones with exactly that pattern and rejects voices, fans and motors. Only finds with at least 70% confidence are shared with the map.

          Species MosquitoNet can recognise

          Wingbeat data: HumBugDB and the Abuzz dataset.

          Getting the most range
          • Close to the sleeper — phone flat, 20–40 cm from the head.
          • Quiet room — fans, air-conditioning, TV and music off.
          • Microphone uncovered — remove a case that covers it.
          • Right time — night for Anopheles and Culex, daytime for Aedes.

          About the map data

          Each circle is one documented site where the vector is present; ringed circles are invasive first detections, dashed circles are populations that are not established. Squares are community hotspots.

          Sources: WHO World Malaria Report 2025, Sinka et al. 2012, PAHO, CDC ArboNET, ECDC and Kraemer et al. 2015 — checked against a land mask and de-duplicated.

          Privacy
          • No audio is stored or sent. Sound is analysed in real time and discarded.
          • Shared: confirmed detections only, with the location moved by a fixed secret offset of up to ~1.5 km, plus differentially private model updates.
          • Anonymous: a random device ID, kept on the server only as a hash.
          • Your history stays in this browser.

          Install the app
          • iPhone, iPad (Safari): Share → Add to Home Screen.
          • Android (Chrome): ⋮ menu → Install app.
          • Desktop (Chrome, Edge): install icon in the address bar.

          Installed, it opens full screen and works offline.

          MosquitoNet

          Hear the mosquito
          before it bites.

          MosquitoNet listens for the wingbeat tones of disease-carrying mosquitoes and tells you which species is in the room.

          • It listens for wingbeats

            Each species hums at its own pitch. The detector picks out that tone and names the likely species — malaria, dengue or West Nile carriers among them.

          • Private by design

            Sound is analysed on this phone and immediately discarded — nothing is recorded or uploaded. Confirmed detections are shared anonymously, with location shifted by a fixed, secret offset of up to about 1.5 km.

          • Place it by the bed

            Lay the phone flat, 20–40 cm from the sleeper’s head, and switch off fans. The screen dims itself while it listens.

          You’ll also be asked for location, used only to pick the species found in your region. It’s optional.

          Microphone access is off

          MosquitoNet needs the microphone to hear wingbeats. Sound is analysed on this device and never recorded. Turn access back on, then try again:

          iPhone or iPad
          1. Tap aA in Safari’s address bar, then Website Settings.
          2. Set Microphone to Allow.
          3. Or open Settings → Safari → Microphone and choose Allow.
          Android
          1. Tap the icon left of the address bar, then Permissions.
          2. Turn on Microphone.
          3. If it stays blocked: Settings → Apps → Chrome → Permissions → Microphone → Allow.
          Computer
          1. Click the site-settings icon in the address bar and set Microphone to Allow.
          2. Reload the page.
          3. On macOS, also check System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone.

          Location access is off

          Location is optional. It lets MosquitoNet focus on the species found in your region and place your anonymous detections on the map (offset by up to about 1.5 km). Detection works without it.

          iPhone or iPad
          1. Open Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services and make sure it is on.
          2. Scroll to Safari Websites and choose While Using the App.
          3. Return here and tap Try again.
          Android
          1. Tap the icon left of the address bar, then Permissions → Location → Allow.
          2. Check that device location is on in quick settings.
          Computer
          1. Click the site-settings icon in the address bar and set Location to Allow.
          2. Reload the page.

          Clear detection history?

          This permanently removes all detections and listening sessions stored on this device. Detections already shared with the map are not affected. Consider exporting first.
